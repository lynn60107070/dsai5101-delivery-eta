05Conclusion

What it means, and what it can't tell us

Answer

At ordering, delivery time can be predicted to about 6.5 min, as well as the app's own estimate. Distance drives it.

The ceiling is information, not the model

Order-time features
6.25
+ real-time features
5.92
+ after-order times (cheating)
4.53

Validation MAE in minutes; R² 0.51 → 0.72 with after-order times. Real-time data closes a fifth of the gap; the rest only exists after you order.

Limits

8 days, one weekend day to learn from · no weather or traffic · free riders estimated from past deliveries · one city, one platform

Ethics

Every minute cut from a promise lands on a rider. Errors also differ by district (4.4–9.0 min).

Next
Confirm the new features on new days; update the estimate after dispatch.

A good promise needs more than an accurate number.

Restaurant or road?

B20Backup

Features we engineered

Main model: 10 features, 33 inputs

Haversine distance · time of day as sine/cosine · lunch and dinner flags · prep estimate + missing and default flags · district load relative to its usual level + flag · district (one-hot, 23)

Extended: 23 features, 46 inputs

+ riders who finished in the district in 30 min · open orders · orders per rider · restaurant's past delivery and pickup times · recent district delays · restaurant queue · prep-estimate bias

Rule for every feature
Only events that had already happened when the order was placed. Tried and rejected: Manhattan distance (same), log target (worse), weekend flag (one weekend day), LightGBM (same accuracy).
