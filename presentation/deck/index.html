03Methods & Evaluation

Tested like real life

The model only sees the past; the test days were scored once, at the end.

Every fold learns only from earlier days
Like the app: it never sees tomorrow. The test days stay locked until the very end.

Metric

MAE in minutes: readable, not dominated by a few extreme orders. Plus RMSE, R² and bootstrap 95% intervals.

No leakage

Imputing, scaling and encoding refitted on each fold's training days.

Dropped

Weekend flag: one weekend day, so its effect can't be learned or validated.

Tree size matters

Bigger trees overfit: 511 leaves, 2.45 train vs 6.63 validation error.

05Conclusion

What it means, and what it can't tell us

Answer

At ordering, delivery time can be predicted to about 6.5 min, as well as the app's own estimate. Distance drives it.

The ceiling is information, not the model

Order-time features
6.25
+ real-time features
5.92
+ after-order times (cheating)
4.53

Validation MAE in minutes; R² 0.51 → 0.72 with after-order times. Real-time data closes a fifth of the gap; the rest only exists after you order.

Limits

8 days, one weekend day to learn from · no weather or traffic · free riders estimated from past deliveries · one city, one platform

Ethics

Every minute cut from a promise lands on a rider. Errors also differ by district (4.4–9.0 min).

Next
Confirm the new features on new days; update the estimate after dispatch.

A good promise needs more than an accurate number.
