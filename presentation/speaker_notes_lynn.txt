SLIDE 11 · Our Approach · ~30 s · 73 words
Now that we have an idea of what drives delivery time, how do we predict it? We set two baselines to beat: a do-nothing baseline, which is the median time for every order, and the app's own promise. Then we trained three models. [Elastic-Net line] Elastic-Net is one weighted line, so every effect reads in minutes. [trees] Random Forest averages many trees, so it's stable. [orange bar shrinking] And gradient boosting adds trees one at a time, each fixing the last one's mistakes.

SLIDE 12 · Inside the models · ~35 s · 89 words
All three models get the same inputs, and each one comes from something we found in the data. [table] We give it these columns here, and that's ten numbers plus the district which was encoded, so 33 inputs. [chips] We left out a weekend flag, because we only have one weekend day, and anything after you order, like the rider. [pipeline] And the cleaning pipeline is refitted in every fold, so nothing leaks.

SLIDE 13 · Tested like real life · ~30 s · 76 words
We tested it like real life: it only learns from the past. [folds building] We train on earlier days and check on the next one, repeat that four times. [lock] The last two days stayed locked and were scored once, at the end. We used MAE as our metric. Tree size mattered in our case: very big trees were off by 2.5 minutes on days they'd seen, but 6.6 on new ones. That's overfitting, so we kept them smaller.
   (If asked: Weekend flag: fold 4 trains on only 14 weekend rows, so it can't be learned; the 6.8 → 18.0 jump was scaling, not weekends (B7) · complexity curve: B6.)

SLIDE 14 · Results · ~35 s · 95 words
[bars growing] These are the results on 140 thousand orders the models had never seen. All three models land near six and a half, and boosting is best at 6.49, 31 percent better than guessing. [orange bar shrinks] The app's promise looks worse because it's padded. Take off its usual 8 minutes and it's 6.48, level with us. Removing the padding per distance band, which is fairer to the app, puts it slightly ahead, 6.36: we reach the app's level, not beyond it (B2).

SLIDE 15 · Distance drives delivery time most · ~30 s · 74 words
So what makes a delivery slow? [table shuffles] We shuffle one input at a time and see how much worse the model gets. [meter] Shuffle distance, and the error jumps from 6.24 to 9.34 minutes, so distance matters most by far. [bars] After that come time of day, the district and the prep estimate. How busy the district is barely matters.
   (If asked: Permutation importance on the CV folds · stages by district: B5.)

SLIDE 16 · Key Insights · ~20 s · 55 words
Distance dominates. Scarce riders go with slow deliveries, but once distance, hour and district are known they add only 0.05 minutes; the restaurant's track record adds the most, 0.24. When we add what the app knows live, we get down to 5.92 minutes, ahead of the app's 6.24. We're better on short and long trips, at dinner and on Saturday, tied on mid-length trips, and behind at lunch.
