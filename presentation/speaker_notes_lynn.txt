SLIDE 12 · Our Approach · ~25 s · 62 words
So how do we predict it? Two baselines to beat: guess the typical time for everyone, and the app's own promise. Then three models, from simple to complex. [Elastic-Net line] Elastic-Net is one weighted line, so we can read every effect in minutes. [trees] Random Forest averages many trees, so it's robust. [orange bar shrinking] Gradient boosting adds trees one by one, each fixing the last one's mistakes.

SLIDE 13 · Inside the models · ~30 s · 79 words
All three get the same inputs. [table] Each one comes from something we saw in the data: distance, time of day, the prep estimate with a flag for placeholders, how busy the district is compared to normal, and the district. Ten numbers plus the district: 33 inputs. [chips] Left out on purpose: a weekend flag, since we only have one weekend day, and anything after you order, like the rider. [pipeline] And the pipeline is refitted on every fold, so nothing leaks.

SLIDE 14 · Tested like real life · ~25 s · 72 words
We test it like real life: it only learns from the past. [folds building] Train on earlier days, check on the next one, four times. [lock] The last two days, including your pizza, stayed locked and were scored once, at the end. Error is in minutes, the mean absolute error. And tuning mattered: very big trees were off by 2.5 minutes on days they'd seen, but 6.6 on new ones, so we kept them smaller.
   (If asked: Weekend flag broke fold 4: 6.8 → 18.0 (B7) · complexity curve: B6.)

SLIDE 15 · Results · ~35 s · 83 words
[bars growing] Both baselines miss by about nine and a half minutes. All three models land near six and a half; boosting is best at 6.49, on 140 thousand orders it had never seen. That's 31 percent better than guessing. The models are within 0.3 of each other, so the limit is the information, not the model. [orange bar shrinks] The app's promise looks worse, but that's padding: take off its usual 8 minutes and it's 6.48, level with us. [pizza card] Your pizza: app 51, us 45, actual 43.
